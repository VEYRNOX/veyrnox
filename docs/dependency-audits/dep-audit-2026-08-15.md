## Moderate / Low (summary)

Zero moderate. All 18 low findings resolve to **one advisory**,
[GHSA-848j-6mx2-7j84](https://github.com/advisories/GHSA-848j-6mx2-7j84) — *Elliptic Uses a
Cryptographic Primitive with a Risky Implementation*, `elliptic <=6.6.1` — reached by two
independent chains:

- **Ledger chain (direct dep `@ledgerhq/hw-app-eth`)** — `@ethersproject/signing-key` →
  `elliptic`, i.e. ethers **v5** vendored inside the Ledger SDK. Also drags
  `@ethersproject/{abi,abstract-provider,abstract-signer,hash,transactions}` and
  `@ledgerhq/evm-tools` into the report. npm's proposed fix is
  `@ledgerhq/hw-app-eth@6.40.3` — a **major downgrade** from the installed 7.8.12; declined.
- **Trezor chain (direct dep `@trezor/connect-web`)** — `@trezor/utxo-lib` →
  `tiny-secp256k1` → `elliptic`, plus `crypto-browserify` / `browserify-sign` /
  `create-ecdh`. npm reports `fixAvailable: false` — no upgrade path exists today.

**Honest scoping:** `elliptic` sits inside hardware-wallet vendor code, on a path that can
touch signature handling for Ledger/Trezor flows. Veyrnox's own key derivation and signing
use `@noble`/`@scure` exclusively and are not affected. The advisory is rated *low* and
concerns implementation risk in the primitive rather than a demonstrated key-recovery
exploit; no exploitability assessment against these specific call paths has been done here.
Tracking it as vendor-supply-chain exposure is the correct framing — not as a Veyrnox
crypto defect, and not as "clean" either.
